# Talatal Ghar: architectural recheck and three views

## Assessment of v1

Recognizable masonry features, but upper ranges too repetitive and balcony-dominated; too much terrace covered; wing geometry and giant surrounding watercourse insufficiently constrained. Not a complete reconstruction of the historic site. Generic vernacular timber/thatch materials alone cannot establish an Ahom royal elevation.

## Material evidentiary correction

ASI's own description (https://asiguwahaticircle.gov.in/, Talatalghar section, accessed 2026-10-31) states that the north-south monument has long annexes at ends and middle, and predominantly open upper terrace except an octagonal small temple, guard room, three large chambers and hall/courtyard. It interprets numerous terrace post-holes as supports for temporary ceremonial shades. Excavated burnt wooden logs and post-holes demonstrate impermanent structures at the site, not the exact floor count or design of the timber upper work. Earlier notes must be read with this qualification. Two added timber floors are the user's requested reconstruction premise, not an independently proven restoration specification.

The India submission at https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/7033/ supports wood/bamboo construction, brick rebuilding and royal upper apartments. It describes enclosure and water-filled dikes; it does not supply measured original elevations. Do not equate all Ahom-period civic buildings with a single pure style or remove real Indo-Islamic masonry features to make it seem more Ahom.

## Revised approach

More enclosed timber-and-bamboo infill, fewer repeated balconies, distinct roof groups and broad uncovered terraces. Warm lime plaster over thin bricks, recognizable low octagonal pavilion and do-chala chamber. Long intersecting irregular footprint guided by real aerial image 107. Omit the oversized moat. Exact roofs, timber floor assignment, room allocation, finishes, ancillary buildings and landscape remain speculative.
