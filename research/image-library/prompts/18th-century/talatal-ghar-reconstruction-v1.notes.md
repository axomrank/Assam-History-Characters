## Design and confidence

- Subject: Rangpur palace / Talatal Ghar, Sivasagar, in its mature eighteenth-century masonry phase with an interpreted timber superstructure. Not the separate Garhgaon Kareng Ghar.
- Surviving masonry geometry, arches, stairs, octagonal pavilion and curved-roof chamber are anchored to local photographs; overall footprint is guided by an aerial photograph and the photographed ASI key plan. Image generation cannot guarantee measured plan fidelity.
- The user specifically requests TWO additional timber-and-bamboo floors. This is the visualization brief. Historical sources corroborate perishable construction and lost upper accommodation but the sources checked do not securely establish the exact geometry or number of surviving-versus-lost floor levels.
- Two timber storeys, post positions, bamboo wall infill, shutters, verandahs, thatch roofs, finishes and their exact arrangement are interpretive. The rendered roof form is not an excavated design.
- Brick fortification, earthen garh and water-filled dikes are documented. Their exact appearance and placement in this image are inferred.
- No underground section or long escape tunnels are reconstructed. The seven-storey tradition is explicitly qualified in India's UNESCO Tentative List submission and is not treated as a surveyed floor plan.

## Sources consulted

1. India submission, Rangpur-Sivasagar Historic Ensemble, UNESCO Tentative List: https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/7033/ . Describes an earlier wood/bamboo palace, brick renovation under Rajesvara Singha, upper royal apartments and fortified water-defended enclosure. Uses qualified language for the seven-storey account.
2. ASI Guwahati Circle, Karenghar (Talatalghar) Complex excavation: https://asiguwahaticircle.gov.in/excavation9.html . Reports excavations in 2000-01 through 2002-03, buried structures to the northwest/northeast, brick platforms, paths, long walls and terracotta-pipe drains.
3. Photographed ASI site board, local talatal-ghar-108.jpg: describes long open terrace with annexes, octagonal temple, Assamese-type do-chala cells, stables and excavated burnt wooden logs/post-holes. The board provides useful physical context, not a full original elevation.
4. INTACH architecture panels search extract indicates possible temporary structures on terraces and filled circular holes. PDF retrieval returned HTTP 403; not relied on for exact designs.
