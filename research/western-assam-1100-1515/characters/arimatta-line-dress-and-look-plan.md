# Arimatta line — character sheet costume and look plan

27 October 2026. Scope: Arimatta, Gajanka, Sukranka and Mriganka. This follows the user's visual direction for the Early Kamata group: keep the darker regional facial direction and woven clothing, with a gradual increase in royal ornaments across generations. The design below makes this later line visually distinct while retaining continuity with Kamata.

## What distinguishes this line from Early Kamata

The names and order of Arimatta, Gajanka, Sukranka and Mriganka occur in later Assamese chronicles and genealogical traditions, but their history is especially uncertain. N. N. Acharyya says the post-Indranarayan succession is unclear, recounts Arimatta/Sasanka as an usurper, notes multiple conflicting legends, and discusses conflicting identifications around Jangal Valahu, Ratna Singha and Gajanka. The sources do not establish a separate ethnic identity, authenticated faces, or a reliable costume system for this line. Acharyya's list assigns Arimatta c.1365–85, Gajanka c.1385–1400, Sukranka c.1400–15 and Mriganka c.1415–40, but treat these as reconstructed dates, not certainty. He notes the Buranji's much broader 1238–1478 date span as chronologically problematic.

For art direction, distinguish them from the Early Kamata rulers through a **frontier consolidation court** visual language: more practical layered cloth, broad and robust wraps, heavier geometric woven borders and modest metal ornaments rather than Pratapdhwaj-through-Indranarayan's increasingly polished, fine, courtly textiles and gold accents. Keep the shared ivory dhoti and shoulder sash vocabulary, dark regional facial direction and muted palette. Reduce visible Hindu court markers to a small tilak and one or two simple ornaments unless the user asks for more. This is a narrative design interpretation of political transition, not evidence that the four rulers dressed this way or had a different religion or ancestry.

## Shared visual grammar

- Eight views per sheet, warm grey studio, realistic skin and handwoven cloth, same face and dress across panels.
- Maintain medium-deep brown skin and the user's chosen regional facial direction, with natural variation between individuals. Do not exaggerate eye shape or imply a biological category.
- Base clothing: unbleached cotton dhoti at mid-calf or ankle; one narrow or medium-width diagonal woven sash; separate shoulder cloth where suitable; broad waist wrap. Cloth should look thicker and more practical than the fine polished Early Kamata court dress.
- Palette: ivory, charcoal umber, faded indigo, iron-red/madder and muted ochre. Geometric diamonds, stepped lines and bands as invented motifs; no unsupported dynasty emblem.
- Minimal jewellery: small brass/bronze or restrained gold earrings, one simple neck cord or bead strand, optional narrow armlet. No tall crown, Mughal turban, armour or abundant gold. Headcloth varies by person.
- These costume details are design decisions informed by user references to regional woven dress. The available historical sources do not verify their use in the fourteenth or fifteenth century.

## Research gates before prompts

1. Read Acharyya, pp. 165–170, alongside the cited Buranji references; record what is explicitly sourced versus his inference.
2. Check the land grant discussed by Jenkins and the chronicle list ordering the names; avoid presenting reign dates as settled.
3. Resolve whether Jangal Valahu, Ratna Singha and Gajanka are separate people or variant identifications before assigning family links or stories.
4. Review the Arimatta traditions (including alleged parentage and death) as competing legends, not factual biography.
5. Do not infer ancestry, religion, court rank or ethnic clothing from the names, facial direction, or modern regional photos.
