## Shared production direction

Eight-view 1536 × 1024 or higher landscape sheets: four full-body views (front, profile, back, three-quarter) and four face views. Warm neutral studio backdrop, realistic skin and handwoven fabric, consistent identity and costume across panels. No dark-circle emphasis, spectacles, modern shirts, neon colours or generic ornate fantasy crowns. Retain original generated masters and save drafts non-destructively under drafts/western-assam/<id>/. Research and prompts belong here or research/ahom/people/<id>/; never beside images. Publish only selected versions, create WebP and thumbnail, update CSV, build site and index library.

Sandhya and Sindhu Rai establish the user's preferred regional costume vocabulary: off-white draped lower cloth, headcloth, diagonal woven sash and geometric waist wrap in restrained madder, ochre, indigo and umber. The modern user-supplied clothing photographs are creative comparisons, not proof of medieval dress. Use the vocabulary as a starting point, vary cut and textile emphasis between periods and roles only as explicit design choices. It does not establish anybody's ethnicity. Do not give every ruler Sandhya's face or presume family relationships from succession.

All ages, faces, colours and accessories below are proposals, not documented facts. Eye shapes should vary naturally between individuals; the user's eye-shape request applied to Sindhu Rai, not automatically to everyone.

## Individual briefs and research gates

### Shared costume construction

Use a simple layered drape rather than stitched court jackets: (1) off-white handwoven lower wrap, knee-to-ankle length depending on the character brief; (2) separate cross-body or shoulder sash, about one shoulder-width, with restrained geometric woven bands; (3) broad waist cloth or belt with a small front fold; (4) optional plain headcloth on rulers where useful for silhouette. Men in the user's photographs wear a bare upper torso beneath the shoulder sash; treat this as an available design option, not a fixed rule for every historical figure. Keep cotton/linen texture matte and practical. Muted natural-dye palette: unbleached cream, faded indigo, madder brown-red, dark umber and a little ochre. Avoid modern blouse/shirt silhouettes, shiny synthetic fabric, contemporary festival saturation, Mughal/rajput turbans, crowns, armour and invented royal insignia. Minimal jewellery: at most a plain cord pendant, small bangle, or no jewellery. Footwear is simple leather sandal or bare feet, chosen consistently per individual. These construction details are a coherent project visual language inferred from the user's modern regional clothing references; they are not evidence for 13th–15th-century dress.

Every image uses the same eight views and studio setup. Age ranges below indicate visual casting only. Make face identity the primary variation: do not signal ancestry through stereotyped features, and do not assume family relationships beyond what sources state. Research must check kinship before family resemblance is added. The visual proposals below are intentionally more concrete than the historic evidence allows, so captions and research notes must call them artistic reconstructions.

## Research method and sources

Start with each person's master CSV record and cited sources. Consult N. N. Acharyya, The History of Medieval Assam (1966), Kamata chapter, especially pp. 152–170; track his named earlier chronicles separately from his reconstructions. The text explicitly warns that history after the middle of the fourteenth century is uncertain (p.164). It discusses Durlabhnarayan–Indranarayan succession and competing Arimatta-line chronologies. Do not turn tentative chronicle lists into exact verified reigns.
