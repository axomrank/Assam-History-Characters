## Political and chronological controls

Kamarupa is treated as politically fragmented, with Bengal intervention and regional authorities. Restrained jewellery and headgear are artistic ways of differentiating the group; weakness does not establish poverty. Royal titles and autonomy are not measurable from crown size.

Timgyadeva and Vaidyadeva reuse existing scene identities. EI II, printed p.355, verses 9 and 12–13 explicitly identifies Vaidyadeva as Kumarapala’s minister and replacement for Timgyadeva; p.356, verse 14 narrates his defeat of Timgyadeva and accession. Its extravagant praise and violent metaphors are court panegyric, not literal instructions for imagery. These pages do not authenticate faces or clothing.

## Design choices

All twelve faces, apparent ages and physiques are artistic inventions, except that two preserve existing invented scene identities. No skin tone or face is offered as evidence of ancient ancestry. Different faces and builds avoid cloning Jaya Pala, whose sheet is solely a layout/rendering reference. Colours and small ornaments distinguish characters without inventing official dynastic uniforms. No exact crown, fibre, motif or garment cut is asserted as attested for an individual.

Baseline designs: Timgyadeva rust/green; Vaidyadeva teal/maroon; Vallabhadeva cream/plum; later-lineage Bhaskara ochre/ivory; Rayarideva indigo/cream; Udayakarna moss/ivory; Samudrapala faded indigo/ochre; provisional Prithu rust/charcoal; Kesavadeva green/ivory; provisional Gokuladeva ochre/indigo; Narayana brick/green; provisional Kharabana ochre/green with plain headcloth. Modern community photographs are not used as evidence of twelfth-century royal ethnicity.

Review verifies all eight angles, facial/costume continuity, full visible feet and hands, and absence of unwanted symbols. Earlier selected scenes and the catalogue remain unchanged during generation.
