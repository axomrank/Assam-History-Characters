# Post-Pala Kamarupa — character-sheet production plan

Prepared 26 October 2026. Scope: twelve current `Kamarupa after the Palas` entries, plus Bengal's Ramapala and Kumarapala as necessary political counterparts. Planning only: no images generated and no catalogue records changed.

## Governing direction

The user's priority is weakened Kamarupa and Bengal's intervention. Depict a loss of unified authority and the coexistence of imperial overlords, appointed governors, rulers asserting autonomy, and regional houses. Do not turn this roster into fourteen successive sovereigns of one intact kingdom.

Political weakness does not establish impoverished rulers, ruined clothing or the disappearance of skilled weaving and goldwork. Use selective fine textiles and modest jewellery for regional elites; reserve the most elaborate proposed court outfit for Bengal's imperial court. These are visual storytelling choices, not documented costume grades. Vaidyadeva's royal claims also require dignity rather than a permanently subordinate appearance.

The Bengal Pala rulers in this phase are Ramapala and his successor Kumarapala, distinct from Kamarupa's earlier Pala dynasty. Do not confuse Kumarapala of Bengal with the Chaulukya ruler of Gujarat. Their connection to Kamarupa should be described as overlordship and intervention through appointed rulers, with the reach, dates and duration qualified; do not imply that either personally lived in or administered every part of present Assam.

## Four related visual families

**Bengal court:** Ramapala and Kumarapala. Fine ivory pleated lower garments, carefully draped upper cloths, restrained period-inspired goldwork, related low crown designs. Deep madder and muted gold for Ramapala; deep indigo and ivory for Kumarapala. Do not copy Pala Buddhist deity crowns, religious hand gestures or halo imagery as literal royal dress. A facial relationship is optional fiction, not an authenticated likeness.

**Governors and emergent rulers:** Timgyadeva and Vaidyadeva. Preserve existing scene identities and costumes. Their clothing can show access to court resources while differing from the Bengal sovereigns. Use one baseline court-administration outfit each; a future autonomy or campaign variant must retain the face. Political allegiance cannot be conveyed by an invented historic badge.

**Brahmaputra regional court:** Samudrapala, Bhaskara, Rayarideva, Udayakarna, Vallabhadeva and provisionally Prithu. Fine but less ostentatious wrapped garments and shoulder cloths, selective short upper garments, small diadems or cloth headbands. Each person must have an individual silhouette. No literal moon crown for the so-called lunar family. Do not claim all belonged to one dynasty or all ruled the whole valley.

**Sylhet-associated local courts:** Kharabana, Gokuladeva, Narayana and Kesavadeva, subject to name/date audit. Related humid-eastern-region draped cotton clothing, narrow woven borders, small gold/copper-coloured ornaments. Warm ochre, green, ivory and indigo distinguish designs. Shared regional material vocabulary does not mean identical faces or an established ethnic uniform.

One person per landscape sheet, normally 3:2, matching the existing high-resolution eight-view format. Neutral grey seamless background; natural pores, hair, anatomy and handwoven materials; even soft light. Upper row FRONT, LEFT PROFILE (nose left), BACK, THREE-QUARTER. Lower row FACE CLOSE-UP, RIGHT PROFILE (nose right), LOW ANGLE, HIGH ANGLE. Align full-body scale and floor baseline; keep feet, hands and headgear visible. Actual camera elevation must differ in low/high views. One outfit repeated exactly across all eight panels. No throne, attendants, map, court setting or handheld props on the baseline sheet.

Small name and CHARACTER REFERENCE title. Footer: MODERN ARTISTIC RECONSTRUCTION. For unresolved figures add PROVISIONAL HISTORICAL IDENTIFICATION in accompanying metadata/brief and, where space permits, the footer. No invented exact reign dates on the artwork.

Avoid later Ahom caps/plumes, Mughal jama or court styling, modern Bengali/Assamese ceremonial dress copied wholesale, tall fantasy crowns, modern machine-fastened garments, generic full plate armour, cloned faces and skin-tone coding of rank or origin. The series-wide political hierarchy is a creative device; do not encode it as a scholarly claim about each person's wardrobe or ethnicity.

## Production sequence and review

1. **Batch 1: Bengal intervention, four sheets.** Ramapala, Kumarapala of Bengal, Timgyadeva, Vaidyadeva. Complete a focused period-dress comparison for the new Bengal designs; inspect all existing identity references. Generate and review each sheet separately; select four identities before later scene production.
2. **Batch 2: later regional line, four sheets.** Vallabhadeva first as the dated-inscription anchor, then Bhaskara, Rayarideva and Udayakarna. Read the full inscription edition, settle name/title associations and avoid a fabricated exact reign sequence.
3. **Batch 3: chronology/identification checks, two sheets.** Samudrapala and Prithu. Provisional concepts are possible with clear labels; definitive historical captions depend on the Ambari/chronicle audit.
4. **Batch 4: Sylhet-associated group, up to four sheets.** Kesavadeva first; Narayana and the other current entries follow only after Bhatera name and chronology checks. The output count can change if an epithet/alias has been catalogued as a separate person.

For every image review facial consistency, true profile direction, head-angle variation, complete limbs/feet, lower-garment construction, closure details, same jewellery in every panel and fidelity to the selected reference. Historical review separately checks identity/role/date; visual quality does not settle those questions.

Store initial drafts in `drafts/western-assam/`; prompts, provenance and review notes stay under this research directory. Preserve selected scenes and source originals. When selected sheets are ready, import by the existing person ids through `incoming/` and `tools/add_sheets.py`; keep masters, numbered versions and thumbnails. Update only the appropriate person row, rebuild site data, then refresh INDEX.csv. No automatic renaming, date correction, new-person addition or publication is part of this planning task.
