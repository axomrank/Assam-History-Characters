## Source checks and limits

**S1 — Vallabhadeva's plates.** F. Kielhorn's edition, Epigraphia Indica V, printed pp.186–187, translated genealogy and endowment consulted. It praises Rayarideva's conflict with Vanga and records Vallabhadeva's feeding house near a Mahadeva temple, endowed with seven villages in Saka 1107. That supports the underlying subjects, not an exact preparatory meeting, royal inspection, building design, cooking routine or grain-delivery mechanism. Military praise is not a measured battle report; do not invent an enemy king, site, troop count or victory pose. 1185 belongs to Vallabhadeva's issue, not a securely dated Udayakarna grant.
- https://jainqq.org/pagetext/Epigraphia_Indica_Vol_05/032559/233
- https://jainqq.org/pagetext/Epigraphia_Indica_Vol_05/032559/234

**S2 — Bhatera genealogy and material record.** DHARMA, Ryosuke Furui's digital editions of the Kesavadeva and Isanadeva plates. Indexed transcription/apparatus and translation excerpts inspected; direct Kesavadeva page fetch timed out. These support a Sylhet-associated ruling lineage but preserve disputed readings, especially the current Gokuladeva entry. The Isanadeva translation mentions Narayana and Kesavadeva, and officials in Isanadeva's own administration. Do not transfer the latter's named officials or edict to Kesavadeva. Consequently PPK-06 is explicitly an imagined ordinary consultation rather than an unverified specific Kesavadeva donation ceremony. No exact date is printed.
- https://dharmalekha.info/texts/INSBengalCharters00096
- https://dharmalekha.info/texts/INSBengalCharters00097

**S3 — Ambari archaeology.** ASI Guwahati Circle's excavation account consulted: occupation phases, brick steps/tank, floors, hearths, ceramics and imported wares. Its phases must not be flattened: an early tank does not prove the exact same structure was functioning in the thirteenth century. Use modest brick features as a conservative setting vocabulary, never a facsimile plan or surviving palace reconstruction. Directorate of Archaeology's account also describes the settlement's later phase continuing from around 1100. Their dating frameworks differ in detail. River exchange is a contextual interpretation, not proof of this particular market or toll shelter.
- https://www.asiguwahaticircle.gov.in/excavation8.html
- https://archaeology.assam.gov.in/information-services/detail/list-of-protected-archaeological-sites-and-monuments

**S4 — Samudrapala.** Research material located through Rajat Sanyal's study and Jnanapravaha research discusses the Ambari inscription, a 1232 dating and religious residents/establishment. Another scholarly reading associates the ritual setting with the Jugijan area. The original inscription edition has not been inspected here. Exact site, plan, sect, personal attendance and ritual actions therefore remain unresolved. PPK-05 is a qualified patronage interpretation. It does not depict a later Ekasarana Vaishnava institution or claim an authenticated medieval building.
- https://www.researchgate.net/publication/361527651_Assam_from_the_Fifth_to_the_Thirteenth_Centuries
- https://www.jnanapravaha.org/download/research/drk01.pdf
- https://abhidhajournal.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Manash-Mazumdar.pdf

**S5 — Early thirteenth-century retreat.** A translated Tabaqat-i Nasiri extract consulted describes a guarded stone-arched crossing, its destruction and the returning expedition's predicament. A damaged crossing is the narrative subject, not a securely located modern site. Expedition size, bridge count, exact route and individual appearances are not asserted in the image. The translated narrative is a chronicle account, not eyewitness visual evidence.
- https://www.jatland.com/home/Tabaqat-i_Nasiri (Elliot/Dowson translated primary text reproduced)

**S6 — Local inscription.** Assam Department of Cultural Affairs reproduces the Kanai Borosibowa inscription and dates it 1205; other historical treatments use 1206. The source records a Turkic incursion and destruction, not a named local ruler or an exact scene. No exact year is inscribed in PPK-08, no Prithu likeness is introduced, and the bridge episode is not presented as a literal illustration of the rock inscription itself. Battle and inscription locations must not automatically be equated.
- https://culturalaffairs.assam.gov.in/portlet-innerpage/protected-archaeological-sites-and-monuments

## Visual and production constraints

Photographic realism, plausible humid floodplain, living timber/thatch courts and modest masonry where justified. No automatic ruined cities or impoverished people from political fragmentation. Royal/civic, domestic and religious structures remain distinct. Temple precinct in PPK-03 must not become the model for every house. No later Ahom monuments, Mughal palace vocabulary, modern machinery, modern fastenings, mixed costume on one person, copied board labels, generated readable Sanskrit or exact-year overlays.

Full prompts and reference order are in prompts.json. PNG drafts remain in `drafts/western-assam/post-pala-scenes/`. Working PPK ids are a research batch convention, not newly activated CSV ids. Review focuses on continuity, separate clothing, natural actions, hands, people scale, props and anachronisms; image quality does not validate historical attendance or identity.
