# Bengal Pala and Bengal/Delhi figures — thirteen character-sheet drafts

26 October 2026. User requested the two missing Bengal Pala figures and eleven missing Bengal/Delhi figures within the 1100–1515 storyline. Existing Ghiyasuddin Azam Shah is preserved and is not counted as a new sheet. Built-in image_gen; one separate eight-view output per current person id. No catalogue activation is part of this draft batch.

## Historical checks

### Bengal Palas

The Kamauli charter edition, EI II, printed p.355, names Ramapala and his son Kumarapala and identifies Vaidyadeva as Kumarapala's minister and replacement for Timgyadeva. It is political/administrative evidence, not a visual likeness or dress inventory. Ramapala's chronology varies: Banglapedia gives c.1082–1124, whereas the project row lists other ranges. No exact reign dates are printed. Kumarapala is Bengal's Pala successor, not the more widely illustrated Chaulukya ruler of Gujarat.
- https://jainqq.org/pagetext/Epigraphia_Indica_Vol_02/032556/416
- https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php?title=Ramapala

### Early commanders and governors

Banglapedia's Bakhtiyar and Iwaz entries and its historical synthesis were consulted. The synthesis identifies the Delhi prince Nasiruddin as Iltutmish's son and places Yuzbak among Delhi-appointed Bengal governors. He is distinct from Tughral Tughan Khan and from later similar-named rulers. Do not rely on every geographical/dynastic label in a synthesis: its description of the 1257 opponent differs from the project's Sandhya reconstruction. Reign and campaign dates require dedicated scene checks. These facts motivate sober command/governor dress, not an asserted uniform.
- https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Bakhtiyar_Khalji
- https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Iwaz_Khalji
- https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php?title=History (early Sultanate sections)

### Fourteenth-century figures

Ghiyasuddin Bahadur Shah is the early-fourteenth-century Bengal ruler, not Bahadur Shah II of the sixteenth century or a Mughal emperor. Banglapedia's account distinguishes his independent and Delhi-subordinate phases. Ilyas/Iliyas Shah founded the Ilyas Shahi house; Sikandar is his Bengal successor, not Sikandar Lodi or the literary Iskandar pictured in the manuscript. Ilyas's death and Sikandar's accession differ between the current catalogue and some references; no exact dates printed on sheets. Their existing son Ghiyasuddin Azam Shah sheet supplies format and broad series continuity only, not a documented ancestral face or inherited uniform.
- https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php?title=Ghiyasuddin_Bahadur_Shah
- https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php?title=Iliyas_Shah
- https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Sikandar_Shah

### Late Bengal Sultanate

Banglapedia's Habshi section identifies Muzaffar Shah among Bengal's Habshi rulers. The research therefore does not depict him as a generic pale Mughal noble: his proposed Black/Horn-of-Africa appearance acknowledges the documented background, but individual facial anatomy, complexion, hair, age and physique remain invented, without caricature. His clothing is Bengal Sultanate court dress rather than an invented African ethnic costume.

Husain Shah's accession is variously dated 1493/1494. Numismatic studies and the published museum coin catalogue provide titles and chronology, not a portrait. Coins without portraits cannot establish a face or crown. Danyal is the Husain Shahi prince/governor in the project's western-Assam narrative, not Akbar's later son Daniyal. His young-adult design is artistic, not an established age. Secondary Danyal accounts and their bibliographies were located, but the original chronicle passage was not fully read here; do not build new precise biographical scenes from the sheet alone.
- https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php?title=History (Habshi section)
- https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Husain_Shah
- https://www.orientalnumismaticsociety.org/archive/ONS_149.pdf (original numismatic research)
- https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/17/The_coins_of_the_Muhammadan_states_of_India_in_the_British_Museum_%28IA_cu31924022932374%29.pdf (museum catalogue)

### Important unresolved catalogue identity: Shah Ismail Ghazi

The current `shah-ismail-ghazi` row places a commander under Husain Shah in 1498–99. A biographical reference hosted by Bangladesh's archaeology portal places the commonly known Shah Ismail Ghazi under Barbak Shah and records his death in 1474. These cannot silently be treated as one settled chronology. The generated sheet is marked PROVISIONAL IDENTITY, has no date or patron claim, and must not prove attendance at the 1498 campaign. Do not automatically rename, merge or delete the entry; research whether an attribution error, namesake or later tradition explains the conflict before editing the historical catalogue. A second namesake is not established by this note.
- https://file-khulna.portal.gov.bd/files/archaeology.khulnadiv.gov.bd/page/7ec91a2e_d7cd_4140_bc50_2061c753a72f/e8809888d389dcac92d4edee77896a19.pdf (biographical reference, printed p.118; not a contemporary portrait)

### R1 — Cleveland manuscript, 1119

Cleveland Museum of Art 1938.301.b, inner top cover of the Ashtasahasrika Prajnaparamita. Object record attributes manuscript to Vikramashila/Bihar and paintings to Nepal/Kathmandu. This matters: it is not a surviving Bengal royal portrait or wardrobe plate. The viewed cover has cosmic Buddhas and attendants, with religious colours, gestures and halos. Used only as period material/art context for the two Pala briefs; no divine complexion, monk's robe, sacred gesture, iconographic crown or halo is copied into kings' designs. Its outer bottom cover was also downloaded and inspected, but proved visually unhelpful and was not supplied to generation.

### R2 — Iranian courtly ceramic, late twelfth–early thirteenth century

### R3 — Bengal Sharafnama, 1531–32

British Library Or.13836, a royal manuscript commissioned for Nusrat Shah. Viewed local reproduction shows literary Iskandar and Queen Nushabah, not portraits of Bengal's earlier sultans. It shows robe layering and headwear but is later than all or most subjects: a nearby successor-court comparison for Husain Shahi designs, much weaker for the fourteenth century and not supplied to thirteenth-century briefs. The fantastical/literary crown, pale painted faces and court staging are not copied. Modern research discusses its Bengali/Persianate artistic relationships; the article's full page returned 403 while indexed sections and figure identification were accessible.

## Design decisions by individual

All appearance, age, physique, palette, ornament and exact garment construction below are artistic choices. Neither ethnicity nor political role determines a single facial template. Avoid using inherited anatomy as documentary genealogy. Existing grey studio sheets supply formatting/material continuity only; the new face must be distinct.

Some downloaded period paintings depict head ornaments or closures different from these proposed outfits. The conservative synthesis remains invention; a museum reference does not authenticate the complete prompt. No exact reign dates or new political events appear on artwork. ## Visual review identity revisions

Initial sheets for Iwaz, Yuzbak, Bahadur, Ilyas, Sikandar, Husain and Ismail borrowed too much facial structure from the Azam Shah format reference. Their v02 sheets deliberately redesign facial structure and facial hair across all panels, while preserving clothes and layout. The initial design table above records the first brief; selected final facial designs are recorded in identity-revisions.json. These changes improve character distinction and are not new historical claims. Iwaz has a broad rectangular face and short black beard; Yuzbak a triangular face and goatee; Bahadur a round face and close beard; Ilyas a gaunt face and long silver beard; Sikandar a broad square face and blunt short beard; Husain a clean chin and long moustache; provisional Ismail a slender face and long sparse white beard. All are invented.
