# Varman dynasty of Kamarupa — character development sheet
Research date: 17 October 2026. Status: preliminary historical brief for illustration; not an authenticated portrait sheet.

## Scope and first character
Use the Varmans of ancient Kamarupa, approximately fourth to mid-seventh centuries CE, rather than similarly named dynasties elsewhere. Start with **Bhaskaravarman**, the best-documented ruler for a first character. A dynasty spans generations: do not give every ruler one identical face or costume.

## Bhaskaravarman: evidence-led character card
- Identity: king of Kamarupa; associated with the title Kumara. Use “Bhaskaravarman” consistently; older sources separate it as “Bhaskara Varman”.
- Historical setting: seventh-century court, diplomacy, learning, and relations with Harsha and the pilgrim Xuanzang.
- Supported character direction: formal royal bearing and an interest in learning. These are public roles and reported qualities, not proof of private temperament.
- Useful scene: receiving a scholar or discussing a document with an envoy. This is an illustrative scenario, not a claim about a specific recorded meeting pose.
- Material reference: Nidhanpur copper plates. Use their actual appearance for document details; they do not show what the king looked like.
- Specific reported ceremonial appearance: Gait, printed p. 24, reports Bhaskaravarman taking the role of Brahma Raja in a procession alongside Harsha. This is a particular ceremonial context, not evidence of his everyday crown or clothing.

## What the evidence does not establish
No authenticated portrait has been established by this research. Exact face, skin tone, height, age in a selected scene, hairstyle, moustache, crown, jewellery, footwear, garment cut and colours remain unresolved. Do not turn descriptions of a population into the king’s facial specification. Do not derive anatomy from disputed ethnic-origin theories.

## Reconstruction proposal — all items below are artistic choices
Create an adult royal figure with a consistent invented face. Explore a wrapped lower garment, a light shoulder drape, restrained jewellery, and a simple proposed head ornament. These are provisional design options requiring dated regional and contemporary comparanda, not verified Varman costume. Use natural ivory and muted earthy colours as an art palette, not a historical colour claim. Avoid elaborate armour until an appropriate reference has been checked.

Sheet layout: full-length front, side and back; three-quarter head; neutral and speaking expressions; garment construction detail; separate copper-plate study; annotation key for evidence versus invention. Keep the same facial structure, garment folds and accessories across views.

Prominent caption: “Bhaskaravarman of Kamarupa — modern artistic reconstruction; facial features and costume are interpretive.”

## Dynasty expansion queue
Research Pushyavarman (founder), Bhutivarman (land-grant context), and Bhaskaravarman first. Check the Dubi and Nidhanpur genealogies before finalising a complete ruler sequence, kinship chart or exact regnal dates. Earlier rulers have much less individual visual evidence; use distinct invented designs with transparent labels. Do not present Naraka/Bhagadatta ancestry as demonstrated biological genealogy.

## Avoid anachronisms
Do not borrow later Ahom royal insignia, Rang Ghar architecture, modern gamosa patterns, Mughal court dress, or fantasy plate armour as Varman evidence. Modern statues, textbook paintings and AI portraits may indicate modern reception but cannot authenticate facial likeness or dress.

## Passages actually consulted
Gait 1906, printed pp. 22–24, from the downloaded PDF’s extracted text: invitation to Xuanzang, reported interest in learning and respect for learned monks, and procession account. Gait is a colonial-era secondary source, quoting an older translation; check Xuanzang’s account directly before publication. Its racial and cultural judgments must not be adopted.

The Epigraphia Indica volume has been downloaded but is image-only. Its Nidhanpur article and plate illustrations still require page-level reading; downloading is not consultation.
