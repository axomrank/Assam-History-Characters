## Evidence handling
The CSV is the project’s scene inventory, not an independent historical authority. Its source links and confidence ratings are retained. Each scene’s record JSON and prompt explicitly identify the evidential limit. Low-confidence scenes are illustrated as cautious, non-specific reconstructions rather than newly certified episodes.

- 01 (revised v2, 18 October): Entirely northern Indian Gupta-period workshop. No Assamese delegation, community clothing or Assam landscape. Kamarupa is mentioned in the inscription only. Northern officials and engraving action are invented staging, not a facsimile or a documented eyewitness scene.
- 02–03: Founding/overlordship councils are imagined staging, not recorded ceremonies.
- 04: Davaka attribution is interpreted; a frontier view avoids inventing a specific siege.
- 05: Cave donation is epigraphic; Surendravarman’s equation with Mahendravarman is tentative. No later monumental Kamakhya structure shown.
- 06: Generic ashvamedha ceremonial phase; living horse, not the killing. Specific king attribution is qualified.
- 07: A western-frontier alarm represents an inferred conflict, not an authenticated Yasodharman battle or portrait.
- 08: Earlier grant known through later renewal; scene does not present Bhaskaravarman’s exact later charter as Bhutivarman’s original.
- 09: Hermitage construction visualised; date/era reading and exact site details remain disputed.
- 10: Return from a campaign is an invented way to express reported success; no captured named ruler.
- 11: Defeat/withdrawal illustrated; Susthitavarman shown alive. Do not infer his death in battle from the image.
- 12: Captivity narrative remains low-confidence. Young adult princes’ appearance, route, guards and location are invented.
- 13: Embassy from the Harshacharita; individual costumes and court staging reconstructed.
- 14: Xuanzang’s visit is attested; private dialogue, interior and exact travel equipment on this visit are not documented.
- 15: Ceremonial roles are literary reports. Harsha’s canopy and Kumara’s white fly-whisk are shown without turning them into literal multiheaded gods. Costumes/crowd scale are interpretation.

- 17: Supplies associated with a Chinese diplomatic episode; no claim of Wang Xuance personally meeting the king in Kamarupa.
- 18: Symbolic empty throne avoids choosing an uncertain immediate succession, death scene or violent coup.
