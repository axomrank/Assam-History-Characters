# Harsha, Shilabhadra, Banabhatta and Shashanka
Research date: 17 October 2026. Artwork: modern reconstructions, review pending.

## Harshavardhana / Harsha
Seventh-century Pushyabhuti ruler associated with Thanesar and Kannauj, conventionally reigned 606–647 CE, and an ally of Bhaskaravarman. Design: distinct mature face, indigo/ivory imperial draped attire and gold mukut. The precise face, crown and jewellery are invented. Religious patronage developed across his reign; no single forehead mark or monk outfit was imposed on the imperial court design.
Source consulted: P. Bhaskar Reddy, “Harshavardhana,” INFLIBNET, Outlines of Indian History, introduction, sources and early career sections. https://ebooks.inflibnet.ac.in/icp01/chapter/harshavardhana/ . Used for basic chronology and setting, not every military claim; its categorical narratives should be checked against inscriptions before scene writing.

## Shilabhadra / Silabhadra
Elder Buddhist scholar and Xuanzang’s teacher at Nalanda; associated with origins in Samatata, in the eastern Bengal region. Not simply a northwestern Indian courtier. Design: an alert elderly eastern Indian monk in plain ochre/brown draped robes, with an oblong manuscript. Visual age about 85–90 is an artistic choice, not a confirmed chronological statement; accounts assigning him an exceptionally great age are not treated as verified anatomy. No Chinese travel backpack, Tibetan ceremonial hat, royal ornaments or deity halo.
Source consulted: Kanailal Ray, “Shilabhadra,” Banglapedia, https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php?title=Shilabhadra . Biographical article read; exact dates of Xuanzang’s study differ between accounts and are not printed on artwork.
Additional discovery lead: University of Calcutta departmental paper “Shilabhadra the Teacher of Hiuen Tsang,” https://s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/calcutta-university/departmental-journals/H00391.pdf . Indexed excerpt only; full paper not consulted.

## Banabhatta / Bana
Seventh-century Sanskrit writer attached to Harsha’s court; author of Harshacharita and Kadambari. Design: distinctive middle-aged Indian scholar, modest cream/rust draped clothing, small tied hair knot, short beard, oblong manuscript bundle. No authenticated portrait or personal wardrobe was established. Do not portray him as a Buddhist monk solely because his patron supported Buddhist institutions.
Source consulted: University of Utah, Ethics of Suicide Digital Archive, “Bana,” https://ethicsofsuicide.lib.utah.edu/selections/bana/ . The page supplies author context and translated selections; no relevant wardrobe inventory established. INFLIBNET’s Harsha chapter independently identifies Harshacharita among the period’s sources.

## Shashanka
Early seventh-century ruler of Gauda with capital at Karnasuvarna in present West Bengal; an eastern Indian ruler active in wider northern politics. Design: distinct broad mature face, short beard, maroon/green imperial drape and a crown differing from Harsha’s. He is presented as a dignified sovereign, not a caricatured villain. Exact reign endpoints and hostile narratives require caution.
Source consulted: P. K. Bhattacharyya, “Shashanka,” Banglapedia, https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php?title=Shashanka . Opening chronology, political relationships and discussion of partisan accounts read. The article specifically notes the limits of assigning personal responsibility for Rajyavardhana’s death. No murder/persecution scene or definitive moral character was inferred for the portrait.

## Attire basis and limits
The earlier dynasty dossier provides the comparative Gupta/post-Gupta vocabulary for draped lower garments and elite jewellery. It does not certify these four men’s exact wardrobes. A museum comparison describes Indian clerical clothing as rectangular wrapped and draped cloth: National Museum of Asian Art, “Introducing the Three Buddhas (and a Bodhisattva),” https://asia.si.edu/research/essays/introducing-the-three-buddhas-and-a-bodhisattva/ . Indexed description consulted; full-page fetch failed. It is comparative evidence, not Shilabhadra’s garment.
Met catalogue discovery lead: “Buddha Offering Protection,” object 38965, India (probably Bihar), Gupta period, https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/38965 . Indexed description concerns simple monk’s cloth; image not downloaded or visually consulted for this batch.

No historical portrait was authenticated. Skin tones, facial structures, ages, body types, colours, jewels and manuscript props are artistic choices for differentiated characters, not proof of ancestry. Barefoot is a series convention.
