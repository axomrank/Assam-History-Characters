# The Chutia and Koch kingdoms, 1350–1616

Research dossier for the site's scenes on the **Chutia kingdom** (Sadiya, c. 1350–1524), the **undivided Koch kingdom**
(1515–1587) and **Koch Hajo** (1581–1616). Written 2026-10-28.

**Labels.** *Recorded*: an inscription, coin or near-contemporary chronicle says it. *Reconstructed*: historians infer it.
*Tradition*: later chronicles or local memory. Each scene row has its own confidence (high, medium, low).

**Honesty about the research.** Assembled in one session from web summaries (mostly Wikipedia articles citing Nath 1989,
Sarkar 1992, Gait, Neog and Choudhary 2024, plus Banglapedia). Those authors are the real authorities; check them before treating
any detail as settled. Where the summaries disagreed, the scene notes say so.

**Scope decisions.** The Koch Bihar line after the 1581–87 split is left out (user decision, 2026-10-28). Events already painted as
Ahom scenes are not repeated; the new scenes tell the Chutia and Koch side and point to the Ahom scene where they meet.

## The one-page version

1. **Sadiya, late 1300s.** In the far east of the valley, around Sadiya, the **Chutia** kings issue Sanskrit land grants (1392 onward),
   build a brick-walled city at the foot of the Mishmi hills and patronise the fierce goddess Kechaikhati, served by Deori priests.
   Their smiths and salt wells make them rich. They are the Ahoms' closest and most dangerous neighbours.
2. **1513–1524.** Border war with the Ahom king Suhungmung ends in the fall of Sadiya. The king dies, the regalia are carried off,
   and the Chutia craftsmen, Brahmans and cannons are moved to the Ahom capital. Queen Sadhani lives on in tradition.
3. **1515.** In the west, the Mech-Koch chief **Biswa Singha** is crowned (KAMATA-27). He counts his people, organises an army,
   brings Brahmins from the Ganges plain, revives Kamakhya, and makes peace with the Ahoms.
4. **1540–1571.** His sons **Nara Narayan** and **Chilarai** make the Koch kingdom the strongest power in the region: a road to the
   Ahom frontier, silver coins, a famous campaign that takes the Ahom capital (AHOM-113), tribute from Manipur, Jaintia, Tripura and
   Sylhet, the rebuilt Kamakhya temple, and a court where **Sankardev** finds protection. Bengal stops them in 1568.
5. **1581–1616.** Nara Narayan divides the kingdom. His nephew **Raghudev** rules the east, **Koch Hajo**, builds the Hayagriva Madhava
   temple and fights the western branch and the Mughals. His son **Parikshit Narayan** loses the kingdom to the Mughals in 1612–14,
   and his brother Balinarayan escapes to the Ahoms, setting up the Ahom–Mughal wars.

## Part 1. The Chutia kingdom (c. 1350–1524)

### 1.1 Origins (Tradition, low)
The *Chutiyar Rajar Vamsavali*, which tells of a founder Birpal, was printed in *Orunodoi* in 1850 and was probably composed in the
early 19th century. A rival origin story (Asambhinna) was recorded from Burmese sources by Ney Elias. The kingdom is usually thought
to have formed in the 13th century, but it only becomes visible in the records in the late 14th. *No scene: the origin stories are
too late to paint as events.*

### 1.2 Kings who left copper plates (Recorded)
| Year | King | Record | Scene |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1392 | Nandi and Satyanarayana | Dhenukhana plate, issued at Sadhayapuri | CHUTIA-01 |
| late 14th c. | Vaishnava Brahmins arrive, write Krishna-linked genealogies | Reconstructed | CHUTIA-02 |
| 1401 | Lakshminarayana | Ghilamara grant | CHUTIA-03 |
| early 15th c. | Lakshminarayana (by a named brick) | Bhismaknagar brick fort | CHUTIA-04 |
| 1428 | Durlabhnarayana | Sadiya-Chepakhowa grant | CHUTIA-05 |
| 1442 | Mukta Dharmanarayana | Wall inscription of the Kechaikhati (Tamreswari) shrine | CHUTIA-06 |

### 1.3 A productive frontier kingdom (Reconstructed)
Smiths, bell-metal workers and goldsmiths; salt springs at Borhat; a royal flag with a peacock (Mayuradhwaja) and heirlooms of a gold
and silver cat, an umbrella and a bedstead. Scholars call it the most advanced tribal polity of the 15th-century valley. → CHUTIA-07.

### 1.4 Wars with the Ahoms
- 1376: an Ahom king, Sutuphaa, is killed in a confrontation with the Chutias → already **AHOM-053, AHOM-054**.
- 1380–87: Tyao Khamti's failed revenge → **AHOM-279**.
- 1513: Dhirnarayan's stockade at Dikhowmukh, routed by Suhungmung → **CHUTIA-08**.
- 1520: the Chutias take the Ahom fort at Mungkhrang at the second attempt → **CHUTIA-09** (the Ahom march is AHOM-095).
- 1523: rout at the Dibru fort, the king killed, the regalia taken → **CHUTIA-10** (annexation from the Ahom side: AHOM-096).

### 1.5 After the fall (1524–27)
- Queen Sadhani, remembered in tradition; the Ahom chronicles name only a chief queen, Nang Lung or Bor Konwari → **CHUTIA-11** (low).
- Brahmans, Kayasthas and artisans moved to the Ahom capital; captured cannons and hand-guns → **CHUTIA-12**.
- Prince Sadhaknarayan sent to Darrang (some sources) → **CHUTIA-13** (low).
- The Ahoms create the post of Sadiyakhowa Gohain (1524), new frontier officers (1525) and the Borpatrogohain (1527), the last said
  to be modelled on a Chutia office. *No scene.*

## Part 2. The undivided Koch kingdom (1515–1587)

### 2.1 Biswa Singha builds a state (1515–1540)
Coronation is **KAMATA-27**. Then:
- census, graded officers (Thakuria, Saikia, Hazari, Omra, Nawab), twelve Mech Karji ministers → **KOCH-01**;
- the court moves from the Chikna hills to Hingulabas → **KOCH-02** (low);
- Kamakhya revived (Tradition) → **KOCH-03**;
- the princes sent to Varanasi; Brahmins from Kanauj, Mithila, Benaras and Puri → **KOCH-04**;
- peace with the Ahom general Tonkham after Turbak's defeat, tribute of horses (c. 1532) → **KOCH-05**;
- the failed march to Singari, and a winter visit to Suhungmung's court (1537) → **KOCH-06**.

### 2.2 Nara Narayan and Chilarai (1540–1571)
- 1540: the princes return and Nara Narayan is king → **KOCH-07**.
- 1547: the Gohain Kamal Ali road to Narayanpur → **KOCH-08**. Pichala (1546–47) is **AHOM-109**.
- c. 1554: the Vrindavani Vastra woven at Barpeta under Sankardev → **KOCH-09**.
- 1555: Narayani silver coins; a letter of friendship to the Ahom king, rebuffed → **KOCH-10**.
- 1550s–60s: Sankardev answers the pundits at court → **KOCH-11**.
- 1562–63: the invasion of the Ahom capital and the Treaty of Majuli → already **AHOM-113, AHOM-114**.
- 1560s, Chilarai's campaigns: the Barak valley and Khaspur (**KOCH-12**), Manipur (**KOCH-13**), Tripura at Langai (**KOCH-14**),
  Jaintia (**KOCH-15**), Sylhet and Dimarua (**KOCH-16**). Years for most are not given in the sources checked.
- 1565: Kamakhya rebuilt, Meghamukdam's brick dome (Recorded, inscription) → **KOCH-17**; the Koch fleet routed → **KOCH-18**.
- 1568: Chilarai captured in Sulaiman Karrani's Bengal → **KOCH-19**; Sankardev dies at Bheladonga (1568 or 1569) → **KOCH-20**.
- 1570–71: the last expedition turned back at the Dhansiri → **KOCH-21**; Chilarai dies of smallpox on the Ganges → **KOCH-22**.
- Undated: an edict recognising Koch, Mech and Kachari religious practice → **KOCH-23**; the epics in Assamese → **KOCH-24** (low).
- 1581: the kingdom divided, Raghudev king of the east at Barnagar → **KOCH-25**.

## Part 3. Koch Hajo (1581–1616)
- 1583: the Hayagriva Madhava temple at Hajo (Recorded, inscription) → **HAJO-01**.
- 1587: Nara Narayan dies; Raghudev stops the tribute → **HAJO-02**.
- 1596: with Isa Khan, Raghudev attacks Bahirbandh; capital to North Guwahati → **HAJO-03**.
- 1597: beaten by a Koch Bihar–Mughal army in May, recovers with Isa Khan later that year → **HAJO-04**.
- 1603: Raghudev dies; Parikshit Narayan succeeds; Man Singha flees to the Ahoms → **HAJO-05**. Mangaldoi's marriage: **AHOM-244**.
- 1603–08: Parikshit recovers the royal insignia → **HAJO-06**.
- 1612: the Mughal army and fleet set out from Dhaka → **HAJO-07**.
- 1613–14: Parikshit surrenders at Pandu and dies; Balinarayan escapes → **HAJO-08**. Balinarayan at the Ahom court: **AHOM-246**;
  the Ahom–Mughal war that follows: **AHOM-247, AHOM-248**.

## Disputes to keep visible
- **Reign dates.** Nara Narayan 1540–1587 (or –1586; one infobox 1554–1587). Raghudev 1581–1603 in one table, 1581–1618 in notes.
- **Kamakhya's destruction.** Popularly blamed on Kalapahar (1568), but the temple was rebuilt in 1565; scholars now date the ruin to 1498.
- **Chilarai's captivity.** One year or several; Kalapahar's role varies by source.
- **Sankardev's death.** 1568 or 1569.
- **Koch Hajo** dates rest mostly on one author (Nath 1989); the Wikipedia article contradicts itself on 1602–03.
- **The last Chutia king's name.** Dhirnarayan in the early chronicles; Nitipal/Nityapal or Chandranarayan in later manuscripts.
- **Sadhani.** Known from 19th-century tradition; identification with the Bor Konwari of the Ahom chronicles is only "general".
- **Bhismaknagar.** Attribution to the Chutias rests on one inscribed brick; the name is modern.

## Notes for painters
- Generated images are interpretations, not evidence. Follow `research/ahom/agent-guidance.md` (no 18th-century Ahom masonry in
  16th-century scenes; keep temples apart from palaces).
- **Koch court:** brick-and-timber, thatch; Kamakhya's dome only from 1565. Koch, Mech and Bhuyan dress: use the project's Koch and
  Bhuyan sheets for continuity; modern Mech and Koch-Rajbongshi dress references are creative direction only.
- **Chutia:** keep continuity with the published sheets of Nandi, Satyanarayana and Dhirnarayan (madder-red woven tunic with cream
  geometric borders, quilted vest, dark headwrap). Sadiya is a river-plain town at the foot of the hills; Bhismaknagar is fired brick and stone.
- **Sankardev** follows his published sheet; Bhuyan men follow the white dhoti and shoulder-cloth direction in the agent guidance.
- Do not invent battle outcomes, casualty figures or cruelty that the sources do not state (KOCH-19, CHUTIA-10, CHUTIA-11).
