## Scene-by-scene guardrails
KOCH-06: supply failure near Singari, 1537. Biswa canonical sheet. Small camp with nearly empty grain baskets, officer and retreating supply bearers; do not merge later Suhungmung audience into one montage.
KOCH-07: return from Varanasi, c.1540 (dating disputed). Nara Narayan and Chilarai canonical faces; change only episode dress to user-requested gerua dhoti/shoulder cloth. No armour, crowns or jewellery.
KOCH-08: embanked earthen road, traditionally Gohain Kamal Ali, associated with 1547 in current CSV. Gosain Kamal canonical sheet; hand tools, baskets, earth compaction, low foothills. No asphalt, stone highway or modern bridge.
KOCH-09: Tantikuchi craft workshop; Sankardev canonical identity with respectful age progression because this is late life, white dhoti and separate left shoulder cloth; male craftspeople distinct faces, same plain white dress. Museum textile plus local loom photograph mandatory. Narrow strip being woven, crimson-ground figured registers, connected warp and cloth beams, supplementary pattern-control cords. No giant completed museum hanging with later Tibetan border on the working loom. Do not add absent Nara Narayan just because he is a named patron.
KOCH-10: mint scene, Nara royal sheet, hand-hammered small silver calligraphic rupees with real size, stone anvil/dies, no modern press or portrait heads. Exact inscriptions only if verified readable reference available; avoid fabricated macro lettering.
KOCH-11: court debate with Sankardev, Nara and Chilarai sheets; do not equate with torture of associates. Modest court architecture is interpretive.
KOCH-12: Barak/Khaspur conquest; avoid surviving later Dimasa brick ruins used as 1562 palace. Date and administrative development need qualification.
KOCH-13: Manipur tribute, show a few representative gifts, no invented exact count or generic Mughal dress for Manipuri rulers. Opposing ruler appearance needs regional research before generation.
KOCH-14: Langai encounter; Nara page explicitly doubts the reported king death and huge casualties. Depict encounter without asserting disputed death. Research regional arms before generation.
KOCH-15: Jaintia submission; Khanga/Nakoidao terms need object references, no arbitrary European swords or universal royal costume. Regional figures not yet visually cleared.
KOCH-16: Sylhet campaign; sources disagree two/three days and have chronology/office problems. Avoid depicting a specific beheading as verified; environment and Bengal arms need direct references.
KOCH-17: 1565 rebuilding inscription/patronage; Chilarai identity, actual temple architectural photo required, Nilachal hill setting; brick dome construction and bamboo scaffolds, omit later additions and modern paint/infrastructure. Do not repeat Kalapahar destruction story as settled fact.

KOCH-24 uses loose bark folios and restrained indistinct marks, not a fabricated readable epic passage.
- Temple morphology checked by directly inspecting TWO real official photographs. District government image prominently includes later hall forms, so the clear Incredible India main dome photograph is selected for 17. Use curved brick courses and older base, without modern fence, wires, solar panel, painted hall additions or urban background. The actual 1565 construction sequence/scaffolding is interpretive.
- Current official tourism prose contains contradictions about Chilarai being king and Kalapahar chronology. Do not repeat those as historical conclusions; use 1565 patronage/rebuilding and existing inscription-qualified notes.
- Local real Majuli country-boat photograph inspected for 18/21. It provides shallow hull and paddle/pole comparison only. Do not call it an authenticated Koch warship plan. War vessels remain cautious reconstructions with no invented identifiable cannon or naval technology claim.
- Pnar and Manipuri current comparative photographs inspected and supplied for 15/13. Plain wraps and restrained borders used; elaborate modern costume, stages, stitched jackets, ceremonial plumes and sacred props omitted. Supporting faces are invented distinct identities; not borrowed from later kings or labelled authenticated likenesses.
- Museum dao records inspected: British Museum As1968,09.14 identifies an Angami/Naga specimen, acquired twentieth century. It is NOT evidence for a Jaintia khanga or sixteenth-century Koch weapon. Therefore 15 represents tribute with coins, horse and a closed weapons bundle; no unsupported close-up sword design is invented.
- Tripura encounter 14 uses simple spear/bow and small cane shield, no detailed royal death (account disputed). Small distant opposing figures use restrained homespun wraps, not asserted exact royal costume. Cloth comparison consulted: https://www.pib.gov.in/FactsheetDetails.aspx?Id=150411&lang=2&reg=48 ; contemporary status cannot prove medieval use.
- Sylhet and captivity 16/19 use restrained representatives and unnamed guards, avoiding unauthenticated portraits of Sulaiman or Kalapahar. Karrani political context checked against https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php?title=Karrani_Rule . Captivity duration and execution accounts remain qualified.
- 20 and 22 depict quiet late-life illness without invented graphic symptoms or specific rituals. Preserve faces with gentle age progression; change armour to plain cloth for Chilarai captive/deathbed scenes.
- 23 uses a representative hearing, not invented readable edict text or claims that all community dress was identical. 24 uses an unnamed scholar rather than a purported authenticated Rama Saraswati portrait. 25 depicts eastern appointment under suzerainty, not later independence or western Koch Bihar succession.

These checks resolve production choices while keeping unavoidable visual reconstruction explicit. They do not establish authenticated likenesses, exact costume, battle formations, lost building plans or functioning boat/loom engineering. Reference manifest records real versus generated inputs.
