# Eight remaining Chutia characters: design plan

28 October 2026. Planning only: no images generated. This plan supersedes the earlier indigo/madder knee-length-tunic, dark-headwrap, quilted-vest and shoe instructions in ../chutia-character-sheets.md and the former CSV sheet_brief cells. The current Chutia man reference and the remade Dhirnarayan sheet establish the clothing family. Individual colours, trims, jewellery and apparent ages below are proposed creative choices.

## Shared visual rules

- Keep the short-sleeve Chutia sula, wrapped Churia lower garment, Tongali waist sash and cloth paguri from the local community reference. Tunics remain around hip/upper-thigh length rather than coat-length. Use distinct garment palettes and border rhythms without inventing separate tribal identities.
- No neck gamusa, scarf, shoulder shawl or chest drape, following the user's express direction. The reference includes a gamusa; exclude it. This is the project's styling rule, not a historical claim that all Chutia elites universally avoided it.
- Ivory/white headcloths preserve continuity; change wrap scale, folds and restrained bands. Never label a speculative tying method as a proven historic Xatphul/Ronuwa type.
- Kings: fine silk appearance with restrained gold. Later royal designs can be a little richer. Kasitora: matte working cloth, minimal metal. Young royals: simple but dignified, not portrayed as impoverished or captive.
- Bare feet in the standard sheet, consistent with the remade kings. Metal headwear stays a low band, not a crown. No Ahom court costume, generic Mughal robes or the old quilted military vest.
- No automatic progression to Vaishnava beads/tilaks. Names and the presence of Brahmins do not establish each individual's sectarian dress.
- Age, build, facial hair, palette, border pattern and exact jewellery are invented design choices. Personality descriptions mean visual bearing, not a sourced psychological biography.

## Historical checks and props

### Lakshminarayana — `lakshminarayana-chutia`

Historical limits: Local records connect him with a 1401 grant. Bhismaknagar associations and the inscribed-brick reading require care; the official gazetteer offers a devotional reading of the words. The builder appearance is a design cue, not a proven personality.

Props: Optional separate inset of a plain fired brick and copper-plate grant; neither held in the standard turnaround, no invented readable inscription.

### Durlabhnarayana (Chutia) — `durlabhnarayana-chutia`

Historical limits: Catalogue records a 1428 grant. Do not confuse with Durlabhnarayan of Kamata. Apparent age, build and costume are invented.

Props: Optional separate copper-plate-grant inset, not a royal sceptre.

### Muktadharmanarayana — `muktadharmanarayana`

Historical limits: 1442 shrine-wall record; readings include Mukta Dharmanarayana and yuva-Dharmanarayana/son-regent. The catalogue calls him king while the older brief calls him prince. Design as a young royal without claiming an exact accession stage or documented age.

Props: None in the eight views; optional plain brick inset only for a later construction scene.

### Pratyaksanarayana — `pratyaksanarayana`

Historical limits: Undated postscripts and variant names complicate identification. Keep this catalogue identity visually distinct, but do not claim a separate authenticated portrait or secure reign dates.

Props: None.

### Yasamanarayana — `yasamanarayana`

Historical limits: Late genealogical identification and possible overlap with Yasanarayana are uncertain. Distinct design is for the separate existing record, not evidence of two different individuals.

Props: None; no prayer beads implying an undocumented religious identity.

### Purandarnarayana — `purandarnarayana`

Historical limits: Listed before Dhirnarayan in the project; few events are securely attached. Martial bearing and sword are creative cues only, not a sourced biography.

Props: A modest sheathed sword at waist, using current Dhirnarayan's project design for continuity; no sword in hand, no claim that he was a documented military ruler.

### Kasitora — `kasitora`

Historical limits: Catalogue describes a minister associated with the final conflict; confidence remains low. Practical soldier-minister styling is interpretation, not a recovered uniform.

Props: Small plain sheathed sword with dark wooden hilt, restrained fittings.

### Sadhaknarayan — `sadhaknarayan`

Historical limits: A prince associated with the fall of the kingdom; accounts differ over his fate. Age 22 is an adult design choice. Neither execution nor deportation is asserted in the costume.

Props: None. Depict a prince in ordinary dignified court clothing, not a captive or impoverished exile.

Supports natural muga's honey-gold appearance, not proof that any particular ruler wore the planned garment.
- [Lakhimpur district: Sericulture](https://lakhimpur.assam.gov.in/departments/sericulture): regional material reference distinguishing golden muga, creamy eri and pale mulberry silk. No individual royal wardrobe claims taken from it.
- [Lower Dibang Valley district: Bhismaknagar](https://roing.nic.in/tourist-place/bhismaknagar/): official description of brick remains, terracotta and inscribed bricks. Its broad site chronology differs from the simplified king-linked account in the older brief; no exact royal construction date is inferred here.
- [Arunachal Pradesh State Gazetteer](https://arunachalpradesh.gov.in/images/Gazetteer/state-gazetteer.pdf): search-indexed epigraphy passage describes animal-motif bricks, a broad palaeographic dating and a devotional reading of the Lakshmi/Narayana wording. Full volume and figures were not inspected. This is a reason to qualify the old personal-brick interpretation, not a basis for claiming a newly settled identification.
- [Chutia kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chutia_Kingdom): secondary orientation to Neog's epigraphic ruler list, competing Mukta/yuva readings, undated names and disputed Sadhaknarayan accounts. Neog's original publication and cited chronicles were not newly read; retain the existing confidence levels and avoid exact accession/age claims.
- [Chutia people, traditional attire and source notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chutia_people): secondary terminology lookup. The cited primary/community books were not directly inspected. Claims about named royal earring/necklace forms, golden footwear, umbrellas and exact headwrap classes are not treated as verified individual wardrobe evidence. Royal ornaments in this plan remain restrained interpretations.

No verified individual likeness for these eight was found in the material checked. Do not describe a generated sheet as evidence.
