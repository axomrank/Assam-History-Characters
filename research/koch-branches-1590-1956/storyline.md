# The later Koch branches: Darrang, Beltola, Bijni and Khaspur (1590–1956)

Research only. These four houses were **taken off the website on 2026-10-28** (user decision); nothing here is in
`data/assam_history.csv`. Their 37 person records, as they were on the site, are in `people.csv` (same columns as the master CSV)
so they can be restored with their sources. Two founders stay on the site: **Balinarayan** (moved to "Koch Hajo kings") and
**Gosain Kamal** (moved to "Koch kingdom (undivided)").

Labels as elsewhere: *Recorded*, *Reconstructed*, *Tradition*. Sources are in each person's `sources` cell in `people.csv` and below.

## Where they come from
When Nara Narayan's kingdom split (1581–87), the eastern half, **Koch Hajo**, fell to the Mughals in 1612–14 (site scenes HAJO-07,
HAJO-08). Its princes survived as small tributary houses between the Ahoms, the Mughals, Bhutan and later the British:

| House | Founder | Lands | End |
|---|---|---|---|
| Darrang | Balinarayan, Parikshit Narayan's brother (1616) | between the Barnadi and the Bharali, north bank | British control 1826 |
| Beltola | Gaj Narayan, another brother (1615) | near Guwahati, under the Ahom Borphukan | mouza after c. 1863; to Assam 1947; privileges ended 1974 |
| Bijni | Chandra Narayan, Parikshit's son | between the Sankosh and the Manas | direct government control 1956 |
| Khaspur | Udita Narayan (independent 1590) | the Barak valley, from Chilarai's conquest of 1562 | merged into the Kachari kingdom 1745 |

## Chronology and possible scenes
Ids are research ids only (not on the site).

### Darrang
- **DARR-01, 1616 (Recorded).** Pratap Singha installs Balinarayan as tributary Raja of Darrang, a buffer against the Mughals,
  after the Ahom victory on the Bharali (27 January 1616). Site scenes nearby: AHOM-246, AHOM-247, AHOM-248.
- **DARR-02, 1637–38 (Recorded).** Balinarayan killed at the Battle of Singari during the Ahom reverses; the Treaty of Asurar Ali
  (1639) follows (AHOM-120 on the site).
- **DARR-03, after the Saraighat war (Reconstructed).** Chandra Narayan installed as tributary ruler after Koch princes help the
  Ahoms drive the Mughals out.
- **DARR-04, 1682 (Recorded).** Mughal commander Mansur Khan captures Surya Narayan and installs his five-year-old brother; the Ahoms
  under Gadadhar Singha then expel the Mughals from Kamrup for good.
- **DARR-05, c. 1785–1789 (Reconstructed).** Raja Hangsanarayan executed by royal troops in the Moamoria troubles.
- **DARR-06, 1791–92 (Recorded).** His son Krishnanarayan rebels, takes North Guwahati with Haradatta, and is defeated by Captain
  Welsh's British expedition (1792).
- **DARR-07, 1826 (Recorded).** Darrang passes under British control.
- Separate thread: Bhutan holds the Darrang Duars from the early 17th century to the Duar War of 1865.

### Beltola
- **BELT-01, 1615 (Recorded).** Gaj Narayan set up as tributary ruler of Beltola under the Ahom Borphukan of Lower Assam.
- **BELT-02, after 1671 (Reconstructed).** Gandharva Narayan, who fought with the Ahoms in the Saraighat war, is installed as king.
- **BELT-03, Ahom period.** Beltola pays the Ahom government a yearly tribute of 2,500 rupees.
- **BELT-04, October 1826 (Recorded).** Lambodar Narayan signs an ordinance accepting the East India Company's overlordship.
- **BELT-05, before 1863 (Recorded).** Under Lokpal Narayan the British reduce the kingdom to a mouza; the raja becomes a mouzadar.
- **BELT-06, 1897 (Recorded).** Chandra Narayan leads relief after the great earthquake.
- **BELT-07, 1937–47 (Recorded).** Rani Lakshmipriya Devi runs Beltola after her husband's death; in 1947 it passes to the
  Government of Assam. (Later: zamindari abolished 1956; Dispur, inside Beltola mouza, becomes the capital in 1973.)

### Bijni
- **BIJN-01, c. 1613–1671 (Reconstructed).** The Mughals make Chandra Narayan, Parikshit's son, zamindar between the Sankosh and the
  Manas; Bijni's rule from Bijni town dates from 1671.
- **BIJN-02, 1864 (Recorded).** An attack by the Bhutanese chief Jhawlia Mech drives the capital to Dumuria.
- **BIJN-03, 1897 (Recorded).** The earthquake destroys the palaces; the capital moves to Jogighopa, then Abhayapuri (1901).
- **BIJN-04, 1956 (Recorded).** The government takes direct control.

### Khaspur
- **KHAS-01, 1562 (Recorded).** Chilarai takes the Barak valley from Tripura (on the site as KOCH-12); Gosain Kamal governs Brahmapur.
- **KHAS-02, 1590 (Recorded).** Udita Narayan declares Khaspur independent.
- **KHAS-03, 1745 (Recorded).** Bhim Singha's only child, a daughter, marries a Kachari prince and Khaspur merges into the Kachari
  (Dimasa) kingdom.

## Gaps
Most Bijni and Khaspur rulers have no dates; the Darrang list after 1682 is uncertain; the Darrang Raj Vamsavali (the house
chronicle) was not consulted.
