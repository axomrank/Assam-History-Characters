# Rivers as kingdom boundaries, by era

Written 2026-10-26. In the data these are the `realm` rows of `data/assam_history.csv` (20 rows). Each names the kingdom, the era, a boundary
written as "West: ... | East: ...", the rivers and landmarks it uses (`places`, which link to place pages in the site), and where sources disagree.
Search a river or a kingdom on the site (for example "Karatoya", "Kamata", "Ahom kingdom") to see them.

**Sources and limits.** Everything here comes from web summaries (Wikipedia articles on Kamarupa, Kamata, the Khen dynasty, Koch Hajo, the Kamrup region,
the Ahom kingdom, the Chutia and Dimasa kingdoms, and the treaties of Asurar Ali and Ghilajharighat) plus one search each on Kamata and the Dimasa. They cite
Sarkar, Gait, Shin, Nath, Dutta and others. Check those before publishing. Several boundaries are traditional or reconstructed; confidence is on each row.

## The pattern

Assam's borders are river borders because the valley is a flood plain between hills. Four lines recur:

| Line | What it divided | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| **Karatoya** (west) | Kamarupa/Kamata from Bengal and Gauda | traditional Kamarupa; Kamata; Khen; the early Koch; Husain Shah's reach |
| **Sankosh** | Koch Bihar from Koch Hajo (1581); today roughly Assam from West Bengal | Koch Hajo, Koch Bihar, Mughal Dhekeri |
| **Manas** | Kamata's lax eastern control; the Mughal-Ahom frontier from 1682 until the British | Kamata, Mughal Kamrup, the Ahom kingdom 1682-1826 |
| **Barnadi** | Kamata/Khen eastern limit; the Mughal limit by 1615; the north-bank line of the 1639 treaty | Kamata, Khen, Koch, Asurar Ali |

East of those the lines shift with the century: the **Bhareli (Kameng)** and **Subansiri** for the Koch at their widest; the **Kolong**, **Kopili**
and **Dhansiri** for the Dimasa and the Ahom push west in the 1520s-30s; the **Dikhow** for the early Ahom-Kachari frontier; the **Sisi**,
**Dihing** and **Lohit** for the Chutias.

## By era

### Kamarupa (c. 350 to 1100)
- **Traditional bounds** (low confidence): west the Karatoya; east Sadiya (the Kalika Purana also names Lalitakanta near Guwahati, and the
  Dikkaravasini and Dikshu, tied to the Dikrong and the Dibang); south the Brahmaputra near Dhaka and Mymensingh; north Kanchenjunga. Texts of Xuanzang,
  the Kalika Purana and the Yogini Tantra, no inscription. Shin (2018) calls fixed boundaries futile.
- **Varmans:** reach rather than boundary: Davaka (Kopili valley), Sylhet (Chandrapuri), Gauda at Karnasuvarna. No river named.
- **Salastambhas:** no boundary found in the sources read.
- **Palas:** a core on the south bank of the Brahmaputra around Kamarupanagara; the west lost to Ramapala; local rulers on the north bank.

### Kamata, Khen and the Sultanate (c. 1257 to c. 1515)
- **Kamata (1257 to 1440):** Karatoya to Barnadi, loose beyond the Manas; Rangpur and northern Mymensingh to the south; the Bhutan hills to the north.
- **Khen (1440 to 1498):** the same span; Nilambar's claimed wider reach is uncited.
- **Husain Shah (c. 1498 to 1505):** the Sultanate reached Hajo by 1502; one account says the Barnadi.

### The Koch (1515 to 1613)
- **Biswa Singha (1515 to 1540):** Karatoya to Barnadi (south edge Ghoraghat in one study).
- **Nara Narayan (c. 1540 to 1581):** Karatoya or Sankosh in the west to the Bhareli, briefly the Subansiri, in the east (competing descriptions).
- **Koch Hajo (1581 to 1613):** Sankosh to Bhareli on the north bank; south of the river west of the Kalang-Kopili.
- **Koch Bihar:** west of the Sankosh.

### Mughals and Ahoms (1615 to 1826)
- **Mughal Kamrup (by 1615):** Dhekeri between the Sankosh and Manas; Kamrup between the Manas and Barnadi.
- **Asurar Ali (Feb 1639):** north bank the Barnadi; south bank Asurar Ali (a causeway at Guwahati, believed to be Rajgarh Road) or the Kalang (two articles
  disagree).
- **Ghilajharighat (Dec 1662 to Jan 1663):** north bank Guwahati to Samdhara on the Bharali; south bank Guwahati to the Kolong; Manas-to-Guwahati returned to
  the Mughals.
- **Manas (from 1682):** the Battle of Itakhuli fixed the Manas as the western boundary until the British; the Borphukan ruled the Manas to Kaliabor.

### The Ahom kingdom through time
- **Early (1228 to c. 1500):** Patkai hills in the east; the Dikhow as the frontier with the Kacharis; offices bounded by the Gerelua and Burai (the rivers
  are not placed here).
- **After the Chutia annexation (1523 to 1536):** east to Sadiya; west to the Dhansiri, then the Kolong in Nagaon.
- **Peak (1657 to 1662):** Sadiya to Kamrup; no western river named.
- **1682 onward:** the Manas.

### Neighbours
- **Chutia (to 1523-24):** the Subansiri, Brahmaputra, Lohit and Dihing valleys; the Sisi in the west; Buri Dihing to the south; Parshuram Kund at the eastern
  end of the north bank.
- **Dimasa (Kachari):** Dikhow (or the Mohong/Namdang) in the east to the Kolong (or the Kopili) in the west; the Dhansiri valley; by the 17th century the
  Dhansiri was a jungle buffer.

## Disputes to resolve
1. Asurar Ali's southern mark: the causeway at Guwahati or the Kalang.
2. Nara Narayan's span: Karatoya-Bhareli or Sankosh-Subansiri.
3. Where the Koch kingdom was divided: Sankosh (main source) or Subansiri (an older mirrored text).
4. The Kachari west limit: the Kolong or the Kopili.
5. Husain Shah's east limit: Hajo or the Barnadi.
6. The Chutia article's own phrase (Sisi in the west to the Brahmaputra in the east) reads oddly.
7. Whether Samdhara on the Bharali (1663 treaty text) is the same fort that resisted the Mughals around 1636.

## Gaps
Salastambha-period bounds; the Burmese occupation (1817-1826); the 1833 settlement (Upper Assam to Purandar Singha without Sadiya and Matak, no rivers named);
the Treaty of Yandabo (its text names no Assam boundary); Jaintia and Manipur; and coordinates for the river lines. The shape files are in `data/maps/`; drawing
these boundaries on them is the obvious next step.
