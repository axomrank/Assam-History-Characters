# The pithas of Kamarupa (researched 2026-11-01)

Sources read: Wikipedia pages on the Kamarupa pithas, Kamapitha, Ratnapitha, Swarnapitha and Saumarapitha (which cite Kakati 1967, D. C. Sircar 1990, the Haragauri-samvada and the Kamrupar Buranji); Yogini Tantra Patala XI (wiki.yoga-vidya.de IAST text); the two boundary-report PDFs.

## What is certain
- Yogini Tantra, Purvakhanda Patala XI vv. 16-18 gives only the four limits of Kamarupa as a whole: Kanjagiri north, Karatoya west, Diksu east, the Laksha-Brahmaputra confluence south.
- The Kalika Purana (10th-11th century) describes Kamarupa as one triangular mahapitha from the Karatoya to Dikkaravasini and names Lalitakanta near Guwahati as a second east limit; no source read gives it a four-pitha river scheme.
- The four pithas (Ratna, Kama, Suvarna or Bhadra, Saumara) are credited to the Yogini Tantra in the secondary literature; their rivers come from Kakati, Sircar, the Haragauri-samvada and the Kamrupar Buranji. They do not occur in the inscriptions and may be a late medieval construction.

| Kamrupar Buranji | Ratna: Maurang kingdom-Sankosh; Kama: Sankosh-Duimunisila; Bhadra: Kallang-Kailash; Saumar: Duimunisila-Dikkaravasini (only Kama listed; Duimunisila has no map point) |
Other Kamapitha readings (not drawn): Vrihadgavakashtantra Manas-Biswanath; R. M. Nath Manas-Guwahati or Manas-Rupahi.

## Identifications used
Bhairavi = Bharali (Jia Bharali); Dikarai/Dikkaravasini = Dikrong (user, 2026-11-01); Rupahi/Rupika/Rupali = the Puthimari/Bornadi (user, 2026-11-01; Pushpita in the Kalika Purana); Silghat = the town on the north bank (26.60 N, 92.93 E). Kakati's Dikarai could also be the Dikrai of Sonitpur; unresolved.
