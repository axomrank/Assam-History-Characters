# Salastambha scenes: evidence and reconstruction

18 October 2026. Eight cinematic stills commissioned by the user. Clothing references are creative inputs, not evidence of exact early-medieval dress or community affiliations. King sheets establish visual continuity only. Scenes remain review drafts, with no catalogue metadata changed.

## Checked sources

- ASI Guwahati Circle, Tezpur rock inscription: https://asiguwahaticircle.gov.in/rock%20inscription.html — attributes the record to Harjaravarman, dates it Gupta era 510 / 829–830 CE, and interprets it as a fishing-area decree.
- Pasupati inscription, digital edition by Dániel Balogh, DHARMA/SIDDHAM: https://dharmalekha.info/texts/INSSiddham02087 — verse 15–16 names Rajyamati, Harshadeva's daughter, as Jayadeva's wife. Marriage is supported; exact wedding date, location, clothing, ritual and father's attendance are not established here.
- Translation of the Pasupati stele: https://zenodo.org/records/1205683/files/IN02087%20CXLII%20Pas%CC%81upati%20Stele%20of%20Jayadeva%20II.pdf
- D. C. Sircar, Epigraphia Indica XXXII, printed pp. 136–137: https://whatisindia.com/inscriptions/epigraphica_indica/vol32_1957-1958/sircar136.html and https://whatisindia.com/inscriptions/epigraphica_indica/vol32_1957-1958/sircar137.html — genealogy, copper-plate donor Balavarman, Vanamala's religious patronage, and later records of Tyagasimha's childless death. Reign dates and dynasty-end reconstructions differ from current catalogue.
- Parbatiya plates study, Epigraphia Indica XXIX, printed p.149: https://jainqq.org/pagetext/Epigraphia_Indica_Vol_29/032583/268 — identifies surviving royal records and corrects earlier genealogical readings.
- University of Lucknow teaching discussion of Yasovarman: https://udrc.lkouniv.ac.in/Content/DepartmentContent/SM_9f47da6e-5f9b-4e5b-9d9f-b232266bdd97_1.pdf — describes Gaudavaho's Magadha campaign; it does not establish that Harshavarman was its defeated king. Source used for caution, not as independent confirmation of SALA-04.

## Scene-specific decisions

| ID | Working title | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| SALA-01 | Salastambha Takes the Throne | Accession illustrated symbolically. No murder of Avantivarman or precise documented accession ceremony asserted. |
| SALA-02 | An Audience at Haruppeshvara | Generalized later-dynasty court with Harjaravarman, not a securely dated assembly. Exact palace site and council arrangement unverified; do not identify Dah Parbatiya as the certain palace location. |
| SALA-03 | Rajyamati and the Nepal Alliance | Uses the inscription's Rajyamati rather than catalogue Rajyavati. Alliance attested; wedding reception, father's attendance and architecture are imagined. Avoid modern Pashupatinath temple architecture. |
| SALA-04 | Harshavarman: A Disputed Last Battle | Explicitly interpretive. The identification of the defeated eastern king with Harshavarman is uncertain. No named killer, exact battlefield, or confirmed manner of death. |
| SALA-05 | Harjaravarman's Rock Decree | Record and date supported by ASI; carving process, observers and king's attendance reconstructed. Decorative script is not an exact epigraphic facsimile. |
| SALA-06 | Vanamala's Copper-Plate Grant | Royal grants attested. Specific presentation, recipient and women in the pavilion are fictional staging. No literal river-god appearance. |
| SALA-07 | Balavarman III's Copper Plates | Copper-plate donor supported. Issuance/workshop reconstructed; archaeological findspot does not establish issuance location. Avoid exact reign date claim. |
| SALA-08 | The Empty Throne after Tyagasimha | Later record supports childless death and succession change; empty throne and anonymous household members are symbolic staging. |

All lighting, faces, gestures, architecture, costume, weapons, staging and crowd arrangements are reconstructed. No generated script should be used as historical transcription.
