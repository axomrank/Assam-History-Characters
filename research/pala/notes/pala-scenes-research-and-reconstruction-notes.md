# Research and reconstruction notes

The sequence follows the existing project's PALA-01 through PALA-11 scene plan. Dates and several political claims in the source catalogue require further historical review; images deliberately carry no date labels or documentary claims.

Madan Kamdev: the Assam Directorate of Archaeology describes stone temples on a hillock, the principal shrine's pancaratha plan and rectangular mandapa, a surviving plinth and ornate human, animal and kirtimukha reliefs. The Kamrup district page places the ruins broadly in the 10th–12th centuries. The workshop, people, scaffold and incomplete upper architecture are artistic staging.

Sources:
- https://archaeology.assam.gov.in/information-services/detail/list-of-protected-archaeological-sites-and-monuments
- https://kamrup.assam.gov.in/tourist-place-detail/218

Costume: approved project character sheets control the named Pala kings. Dharma Pala and Jaya Pala use their revised dhoti sheets. The supporting cast uses the user's modern community clothing as artistic inspiration; no claim is made that these exact costumes or named communities are documented in the depicted events.

PALA-08 depicts the campaign through Jayapala receiving news of an approaching force. It does not assert a personal meeting, duel or surrender to Ramapala.

PALA-09 introduces a fictional reconstructed Timgyadeva. PALA-10 introduces a fictional reconstructed Vaidyadeva; PALA-11 reuses his generated appearance. No approved character sheets existed for these two.

PALA-10 stages the incoming administration, not an undocumented execution or duel. PALA-11 places grant issuance in Kamarupa, rather than confusing the later Kamauli findspot with the court location. No disputed exact date is inscribed on the image.

The timber architecture, royal seats, ceremonies, tools and physical arrangements are speculative. Image-generated incised charter markings are decorative and must not be read as authentic Sanskrit transcriptions.
